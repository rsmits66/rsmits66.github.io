What panel 1 is really testing

Orthogonality is cancellation

Two functions are orthogonal when their product has as much area below the axis as above. Nothing subtler than that. Watch the bronze running integral climb through the crimson lobes and sink through the charcoal ones, landing back on zero at x = b.

The diagonal of the Gram matrix is different in kind: there m = n, the product is φn², which is never negative, so nothing can cancel and the value is ‖φn‖².

Switch to Monomials and try m = 0, n = 2. The product is x², all of it above the axis — the area is 2/3, nowhere near zero. These two directions overlap, and the consequences show up in panel 2.

The experiment that justifies the whole theory

When the formula stops working

With Legendre, press both buttons. The coefficients and the error come out identical, to every digit. Orthogonality is exactly the condition that makes the one-at-a-time formula solve the full least-squares problem.

Now switch to Monomials, 6 terms, and press both. The generalized Fourier formula is now visibly worse — it ignores the overlap between x⁰ and x², x¹ and x³, and so double-counts. Only the normal equations get it right.

Tick normalize with monomials. Every diagonal entry becomes 1, but the off-diagonals stay: normalized is not orthonormal. Note the condition number, too — orthogonality is not decoration, it is what keeps the system solvable.

Best approximation in the mean

Why perpendicular means best

In panel 3 the shaded plane is every function you can build from φm and φn. The target f sticks up out of it, and no choice of coefficients can reach it.

Move your sliders and the residual tips over; press drop the perpendicular and it stands straight up. That is the whole content of least squares: the error is smallest exactly when what is left over is orthogonal to everything you were allowed to use.

At that moment ‖f‖² = ‖p*‖² + ‖f − p*‖² — Pythagoras, in a space of functions. The panel checks the arithmetic for you. For an orthogonal basis the middle term splits further into Σ cn²‖φn‖², which is Parseval.
