Vibrating Strings, Elastic Bars, and Resonance

Second order in time, so two initial conditions — and the modes oscillate for ever instead of dying away.
The problem, and how it differs from heat flow length 1, wave speed c
utt + 2γ ut = c² uxx + F(x) cos Ωt,    u(x, 0) = f(x),   ut(x, 0) = g(x)

Separating u = φ(x)q(t) gives the same eigenvalue problem −φ″ = λφ as the heat equation — the boundary conditions choose the same shapes. What changes is the time factor. Instead of T′ = −αλT we get

qn″ + 2γ qn′ + ωn² qn = Fn cos Ωt,    ωn = c√λn

a second-order oscillator rather than a first-order decay. Hence two initial conditions per mode, and hence — with γ = 0 — motion that never stops.

string · transverse

Tied down: u = 0

φn = √2 sin nπx,   ωn = cnπ
Both ends fixed. The frequencies are exact integer multiples of the fundamental, which is why a plucked string sounds like a musical note rather than a clang.
elastic bar · longitudinal

Traction free: ux = 0

fixed–free: φn = √2 sin(n−½)πx,
ωn = c(n−½)π
A free end carries no stress, σ = E ux = 0. A bar clamped at one end has quarter-wavelength modes in the ratio 1 : 3 : 5, not 1 : 2 : 3.
the case with a surprise

Free at both ends

λ = 0 is an eigenvalue, with φ ≡ 1 and ω = 0
Nothing holds the bar in place, so it can translate. That mode obeys q″ = 0: give the bar net momentum and it drifts away for ever while still vibrating. Compare heat, where λ = 0 conserved the mean temperature instead.
the contrast worth stating

Energy, not decay

E = ½∫(ut² + c²ux²) dx
With γ = 0 this is exactly conserved — kinetic and potential trade back and forth while the total holds still. Heat's energy could only fall. That single sign difference is what separates diffusion from vibration.

The two initial conditions

Pluck versus strike

A plucked string starts displaced and at rest: f is the triangle, g = 0, so every mode starts at its extreme with zero velocity and qn = an cos ωnt.

A struck string starts flat and moving: f = 0, g is the hammer blow, so every mode starts at zero displacement with velocity and qn = (bn/ωn) sin ωnt. Note the extra 1/ωn: striking weights the low modes more heavily than plucking does, which is why a struck string sounds rounder.

Pluck at the midpoint and the even modes vanish — sin(nπ/2) = 0 for even n. Slide the position control to 0.5 and watch every other bar in the Modes tab drop out. Pluck at 1/3 and every third mode goes instead.

Resonance

Three regimes in one slider

Press Resonance: forcing matched to mode 1, Ω tuned to ω1, no damping. The amplitude grows linearly in t — the response is (F/2ω)·t·sin ωt, not a bounded oscillation. Nothing in the equation stops it.

Now detune Ω slightly. The growth is replaced by beats: the exact response is proportional to cos Ωt − cos ω1t, which is a fast oscillation inside a slow envelope of period 2π/|ω1 − Ω|. As the detuning shrinks, the beat period lengthens, and in the limit it becomes the linear growth.

Finally add damping. The secular growth is capped at Fn/(2γωn), and the resonance curve in the Forcing tab turns from a pole into a peak of finite height. Small γ, tall peak.

Strings against bars

Why the ratios matter

Fixed at both ends, ωn = cnπ — the overtones are exact integer multiples, a harmonic series, and the ear hears a pitch.

Clamp a bar at one end and leave the other free and you get ωn = c(n−½)π, ratios 1 : 3 : 5 : 7. Switch the x = 1 control to free and watch the frequency ladder in the Modes tab restretch itself.

Free at both ends, the ladder keeps the 1 : 2 : 3 spacing but gains ω0 = 0 at the bottom. Set ut(x,0) to uniform with both ends free: the bar vibrates and sails off, because nothing is there to absorb its momentum.

The energy tab

Conserved, therefore unique

Differentiating E = ½∫(ut² + c²ux²) and integrating by parts gives

E′ = c²[utux]₀¹ − 2γ∫ut²

The bracket dies at a fixed end (ut = 0 there) and at a free end (ux = 0), so with γ = 0 we get E′ = 0 exactly.

For uniqueness, two solutions with the same data differ by a w with f = g = 0, hence E(0) = 0. Since E is conserved and non-negative, E ≡ 0, so both wt ≡ 0 and wx ≡ 0 — w is constant, and zero initially, so w ≡ 0. Note this needs both terms of E to vanish; the heat argument only had one. Numbers in the tab are computed, not proved.
