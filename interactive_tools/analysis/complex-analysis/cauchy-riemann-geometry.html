Cauchy–Riemann as geometry

$f=u+iv$ is a map $(x,y)\mapsto(u,v)$. Cauchy–Riemann says its Jacobian is a rotation times a scaling.

1Equations

If $f'(z_0)$ exists, every direction of approach gives the same limit.

East, $\Delta z=\Delta x$:

$$f'(z_0)=\E{u_x}+i\,\E{v_x}$$

North, $\Delta z=i\,\Delta y$:

$$f'(z_0)=\N{v_y}-i\,\N{u_y}$$
$$u_x=v_y,\qquad u_y=-v_x$$

2Arrows

Let $\fx=u_x+i\,v_x$ and $\fy=u_y+i\,v_y$: the velocities of $f(z)$ as $z$ moves east and north.

Then $f'(z_0)=\fx=\fy/i$, so

$$\fy=i\,\fx$$

The north arrow is the east arrow turned $90^\circ$ counterclockwise.

3Matrix

The columns of the Jacobian are $\fx$ and $\fy$. With $a+ib=f'(z_0)=re^{i\theta}$:

$$J=\begin{pmatrix}\E{u_x}&\N{u_y}\\ \E{v_x}&\N{v_y}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}a&-b\\ b&a\end{pmatrix}$$

That is rotation by $\theta$ and scaling by $r$:

$$f(z)\approx f(z_0)+f'(z_0)\,(z-z_0)$$

So $f$ is conformal where $f'(z_0)\neq 0$.
