Part 2 · Star-shaped sets

Convex and star-shaped sets

Convex

For any a and b in G, the segment [a, b] lies in G.

Star shaped

Some point a sees all of G: for every z in G, the segment [a, z] lies in G.

Not star shaped

In an annulus, every a has some z whose segment to a crosses the hole.

Convex implies star shaped. Star shaped implies connected: [z, a, w] is a polygon in G from z to w.

Part 3 · Cauchy’s Theorem

A plausible argument, and its gap

  1. Fix w outside G, and a homotopy Γ from a constant curve γ₀ to γ₁ = γ.
  2. Let h(t) be the winding number about w of the stage at time t.
  3. Show h is continuous. It is integer valued, so it is constant.
  4. Since h(0) = 0, we get n(γ; w) = 0, and the first version applies.

The gap

The winding number is an integral along the stage. For 0 < t < 1 the stage need not be rectifiable, so h(t) may not be defined.

1 · Compactness

One scale for the whole square

3 · Cancellation

Shared edges drop out

Neighbouring polygons run along their common edge in opposite directions. Only the two boundary curves survive.

Part 5 · Simply connected regions

Which regions are simply connected?

Star shaped

Yes. Every closed curve shrinks to the centre, by Proposition 6.4.

Slit plane

Yes. The domain of the principal logarithm is star shaped about 1.

Spiral removed

Yes. Remove the spiral r = θ. Its complement in the extended plane, the spiral with ∞, is connected.

Punctured plane

No. The unit circle is not homotopic to zero, and {0, ∞} is not connected.

The spiral example is easy to believe, but a rigorous proof waits for the Chapter VIII criterion.
