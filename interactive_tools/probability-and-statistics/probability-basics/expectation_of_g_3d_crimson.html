The Expected Value of a Function of Random Variables

One equation, two pictures: an average of heights, and a signed volume.
The statement
If Y1, Y2, …, Yk are continuous random variables with joint density f(y1, …, yk), then the expected value of any function g of them is found by weighting g by the density and integrating over all of space:
E[g(Y1,…,Yk)]  =  ∫₋∞^∞⋯∫ g(y1,…,yk) f(y1,…,yk) dy1⋯dyk
Below is the case k = 2, where the whole thing fits in a picture:
E[g(Y1,Y2)]  =  ∬ g(y1,y2) f(y1,y2) dy1dy2

Reading the left stage

Why an average of heights

Every draw (y1, y2) lands somewhere on the floor. The stem above it is tall exactly when g is large there, so the cloud of stem tops traces out the g surface — but only where f actually puts points.

Average those n heights and you get E[g], the gold level. Nothing here mentions an integral: this is the definition of expectation as a long-run average.

Try g = y₁. Stems on the left half hang below the floor (negative), those on the right stand above, and they cancel to a level of 0 — because E[Y₁] = 0.

Reading the right stage

Why a volume

Chop the plane into cells of area Δy₁Δy₂. The chance of landing in one cell is about f · Δy₁Δy₂, and multiplying by the value of g there gives that patch's contribution to the average — the volume of one column.

Add every column and you have the integral. Slide grid cells and notice the total barely moves: each column already carries its own patch's full contribution, so what refining buys you is resolution — the staircase settling into the true shape of g·f.

Notice what the density does to the tails: out at the corners g may be enormous, yet the columns are flat, because f has already gone to nearly zero. Weighting is the whole content of the theorem.

Things worth trying

Four experiments

g = y₁·y₂ — the answer is ρ. Drag the correlation slider and watch crimson columns in two opposite quadrants trade places with charcoal ones. That trade is covariance.

g = y₁² — the answer is 1, the variance of Y1. A moment is just a particular choice of g.

g = 1 if y₁ > 1 — the surface is a flat step and the answer is P(Y₁ > 1) ≈ 0.1587. Probability is the expectation of an indicator.

Switch f to the two-cluster mixture with g unchanged. The surface never moves; the answer does. E[g] depends on f as much as on g.
